# Zephyr language for users

Zephyr is for nontechnical users. Lead with the answer in ordinary business
language, using the few numbers needed to understand it.

- Explain what a record represents when the saved fields establish it. For
  weekly sales by region/category/channel, say "weekly sales records" and name
  records using the week and those group values. Use actual names or IDs for
  other files when available. Keep the analysed-file position secondary. Never
  invent a customer/company name or imply that a descriptive label is unique.
- Start group comparisons with who leads and by how much, before the list of
  figures. State ties honestly, including ties outside the displayed results.
  Say "Store 20" rather than just "20". A single displayed or matching group
  is not proof that it outperforms other groups.
- Count-chart captions must say what is counted: "Number of entries in each
  region" or "Number of entries in each revenue range". Explain an entry as one
  line of data in the report. Do not imply these are unique customers or
  organisations unless a distinct-value calculation establishes that basis.
- Range charts should show readable amount ranges, entry counts on the bars
  and a short explanation of the most common range. Use horizontal bars when
  narrow screens would hide labels. Longer bars mean more entries, not a larger
  total amount. Count axes must never use currency formatting. Disclose rounded
  range limits and any 10,000-entry chart cap.
- Action titles should name the field and, when relevant, the exact measured
  value. Say what to open, what source to check and what decision that check
  supports. Keep expected differences and optional blanks as valid possibilities.
- Forecasts should include the latest figure as a reference, a possible range,
  and a practical next step. Explain when the estimate is a monthly total across
  entries rather than an individual week's result. Do not turn an uncertain
  estimate into a definite rise/fall or a promised financial benefit.
- Say "Total revenue is 12,285,143.73." Avoid "sums to", "supplied values",
  "stored units", query expressions, and repeated versions of the same result.
- Keep formulas, source paths and technical checks in internal evidence or
  optional explanations. They should not appear in a normal chat reply.
- Do not add the file's row and field counts to every answer. Include counts
  when requested or needed to understand a share or comparison.
- Preserve qualifications that affect decisions. Explain them simply:
  "This is based on the first 936 entries, so it may not represent the whole file."
  "Blank cells were filled during cleaning; those values were not in the original file."
- Keep field meaning, currency, percentages and weighting accurate. Never make
  wording easier by relabelling revenue as profit, guessing currency, or treating
  repeated IDs as separate customers.
- If the report cannot answer a question, say so briefly and suggest a useful
  question it can answer. Do not describe internal query or storage limitations.

Plain wording must retain measured evidence. AI may select measured claims;
it must not invent a more appealing answer or unsupported business advice.

The same rules apply to summaries, insights, recommendations, forecasts, file
checks, shared reports and exports. Changing only chat is not sufficient.

- Use "What stands out", "What to do next", "Looking ahead" and "What to check
  in your file" as section headings. Explain the finding before any technical detail.
- Recommendations should say what to check, what was found and why checking
  helps. Use "What we found" and "Why this helps" rather than "Evidence" and
  "Decision supported". Describe priorities as "Check first", "Worth reviewing"
  or "For reference".
- Forecasts are approximate planning guides. Give a rounded estimate and a
  range, keeping the saved precise values internally. State when a rise or fall
  cannot be established. Do not present a confidence score as the probability
  that the estimate will happen.
- Explain file checks through blanks, repeated entries and unusual numbers.
  Unusual numbers may be correct; blank fields may be optional. Show the check
  score only in optional details and explain its limits in everyday language.
- Plain language must still be specific. Do not say "13 numbers are much higher
  or lower than others". Name the measure, show the comparison limits and give
  separate lower/higher counts when available. If an older report does not have
  the split, state the known limits without inventing which side each entry is on.
  Explain that these review limits come from the file, not a business target.
- Keep a single entry's unusual value attached to that entry. Include enough
  readable identifying details to help someone find it, without claiming it is
  the result for the entire region or group.
- Offer "View affected entries", "View supporting entries" and "See figures
  behind this action" when saved evidence supports those controls. Show amounts,
  dates and identifying details before less useful fields. Explain that entry
  numbers refer to the analysed copy when cleaning may have changed positions.
- Comparisons must name the measure and operation: "Total revenue" or "Average
  revenue per entry". Do not add identifiers or serial numbers as performance
  measures, sum rates, guess currency, or stack averages as totals. Calculated
  chat replies use verified results and may link to their supporting entries.
- Do not claim an action will increase revenue or save money unless that benefit
  has been measured. Supporting figures describe the finding, not an invented
  estimate of the action's impact.

- Time charts must identify the first recorded date and amount. Align the line with that date, fit the value axis to the actual amounts, and never invent an earlier zero observation. Use straight segments so the chart does not imply unmeasured peaks.

Revenue chart correction: month-year axis labels (for example Feb 2025) retain the underlying weekly observations and exact tooltip dates. The time-series scale fits the observed amounts, removing the unnecessary empty space down to zero.

- For admission-score files, describe applicant records and recorded admission chances. A mean recorded chance is not an observed acceptance rate. Show score scales and group sizes, explain research codes, and do not turn a valid low score into a correction task. Correlation does not establish a cause or an individual admission prediction. Rule-generated recommendations must not invent an owner or deadline.

- Published survey tables contain group-level results, not individual respondents. Name the selected question, answer and survey group in every percentage chart. Do not average unrelated questions, infer population averages without denominators/weights, or treat blank optional notes as errors. Unpublished percentages remain unavailable, never zero or an imputed estimate.
